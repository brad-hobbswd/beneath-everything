Chapter Five

Beneath Everything Is My Choice

Beneath everything he had experienced, Joseph still possessed the ability to choose what came next.

Joseph Bennett had spent enough of his life waiting for something outside of him to change before believing that he could move forward. There were times when he thought life would become easier once another person understood him, once a difficult situation finally reached its conclusion, once grief became less painful, or once he felt more certain about what the future might hold. As he looked back across the experiences that had brought him to this point, however, Joseph began to recognize that life had never waited for him to feel completely prepared before placing another decision in front of him. Difficult circumstances had arrived without asking whether he was ready, relationships had changed without allowing him to predict every outcome, and losses had occurred without providing him enough time to understand how much they would affect him before they became part of his story.

The experiences behind him had changed the way Joseph understood himself and the world around him, and he did not deny that they had left their mark. Trauma had influenced the way he experienced safety and relationships, while chronic depression had shaped many of the ordinary days through which he continued trying to move forward. Fear had often appeared before Joseph had even decided whether something was worth pursuing, particularly when the possibility in front of him required him to be seen, to take a risk, or to accept that another person might not understand what he was trying to do. His divorce had taken him through an ending he had never expected to face, and the loss of his dog had reminded him that even the relationships and routines he valued could change in ways he had not anticipated. Meeting someone new had shown him that the future could also move in directions he had never planned, and that not every possibility he feared was destined to become reality.

As Joseph considered everything that had happened, he began to understand that none of those experiences had removed his ability to choose what he would do with the life that remained in front of him. They had influenced him, shaped parts of his perspective, and created memories that he would continue carrying, but they had not written the final chapter for him. Somewhere beneath the experiences he had survived, beneath the questions that had followed him and the fear that had often influenced his decisions, there remained a part of Joseph that still wanted something more from life than simply surviving what had already happened. That part of him had never completely disappeared, although there had been times when Joseph had become so focused on other people's needs, difficult circumstances, and the consequences he imagined around every decision that he had given very little attention to what might still be waiting within himself.

Joseph had talents, ideas, and abilities that had not disappeared simply because his life had become complicated. They had remained with him through the difficult seasons, sometimes quietly and sometimes returning with enough persistence that he could no longer pretend they were not there. The difficulty was not that Joseph lacked anything to offer, but that revealing what he possessed required him to allow himself to become visible. He could keep an idea private and protect it from criticism, or he could keep a talent undeveloped and avoid the possibility of discovering how other people might respond to it, but Joseph was beginning to understand that remaining hidden was not the same as remaining safe. There was a certain protection in never allowing himself to be seen, yet that protection could gradually become a place where possibility remained untouched because fear had convinced him that waiting was wiser than beginning.

Fear had rarely needed to convince Joseph that he was incapable. It was often more effective when it suggested that he should simply wait until he felt more confident, more prepared, or more certain about how people would respond. There always seemed to be another reason to postpone beginning, particularly when beginning required Joseph to accept that he could not control every reaction or predict every outcome. As he reflected on how often he had delayed opportunities while waiting for a version of himself who felt completely ready, Joseph began to recognize that life might never provide the perfect conditions he had imagined. If he continued waiting until every fear disappeared before allowing himself to move, he might spend his life preparing for a moment that would never arrive.

Courage did not necessarily require uncertainty to disappear before action became possible.

This realization began changing the way Joseph understood courage. He had once imagined courage as the absence of fear, but his own experiences had taught him that fear could remain present even when a person decided to take another step. Courage did not necessarily require uncertainty to disappear before action became possible, because a person could recognize fear, understand where it came from, and still decide that fear would not be given the authority to determine every direction their life could take. For Joseph, that understanding became particularly important because he had spent so much of his life allowing concern about other people's opinions and reactions to influence decisions that should have required him to consider his own future as well.

Joseph could not control what had happened to him, and he could not change the experiences that had already become part of his history, but he was beginning to understand that he could still choose what he would do with the life that remained. This did not require him to deny his trauma, pretend that depression had never affected him, or convince himself that fear was not real. Instead, it required him to acknowledge those realities without surrendering every possibility in his future to them. He could recognize that fear existed while refusing to allow it to become the final voice in every decision, and he could accept that his past had affected him without believing that it possessed the authority to determine everything he would become.

As Joseph began thinking more seriously about his talents, he noticed how often he had placed them somewhere in the future. He imagined that he would develop them when life became less complicated, when he felt more secure, or when he no longer worried about what people might think. Yet the more honestly he considered that pattern, the more he realized that he had been waiting for circumstances to provide permission that could only come from his own willingness to begin. His talent did not require him to become a different person before he could use it, and the experiences that had made life difficult did not mean that he had nothing meaningful left to offer.

Joseph began to understand that the idea of burying fear did not mean pretending fear had never existed. Fear had been part of his experiences for too long for him to honestly claim otherwise, but he no longer wanted it to remain on the surface of every decision, standing between him and everything he hoped to explore. To bury fear meant that he could place it beneath his choice rather than allowing it to stand above his life, because fear could remain part of his experience without becoming the foundation upon which every future decision was made.

This was where the meaning of revealing his talent became clearer. Joseph was not attempting to prove that he had overcome every painful experience before allowing himself to create, grow, or pursue something meaningful. He was learning that what had been given to him could exist alongside everything he had survived. His history could explain why certain steps were difficult without proving that those steps were impossible, and the opinions of other people could matter without becoming the authority that determined whether Joseph was allowed to develop into more of the person he believed he could become.

Beneath everything is my choice.

As he continued considering these things, Joseph returned to the phrase that had gradually become part of his understanding of himself: Beneath everything is my choice. It was not a statement that denied the influence of depression, trauma, grief, fear, or loss, because Joseph knew too well that a person could not simply choose to erase what had shaped them. Instead, it reminded him that even when he could not control the presence of every difficult thought or emotion, he could still consider what authority those experiences would have over the decisions he made next.

There had been many times when Joseph gave more grace to other people than he allowed himself to receive. He could understand another person's complicated history and recognize that their mistakes did not represent the entirety of who they were, yet he often approached his own life as though he needed to prove that he deserved the opportunity to move forward. The more he reflected on this difference, the more he understood that extending grace toward himself was not an excuse to avoid responsibility. It was an acknowledgment that growth could not happen when every past experience was continually used as evidence against the possibility of his future.

Joseph knew that choosing differently would not become a single decision that permanently changed the way he experienced fear. The choice would return repeatedly, particularly in ordinary moments when uncertainty suggested that remaining still would be safer than trying something new. He would encounter it when he wondered what other people might think, when a difficult memory made him question his ability to move forward, and when the possibility of failure made the familiar choice of remaining hidden appear more comfortable. What had changed was that Joseph was beginning to recognize those moments as opportunities to decide rather than as automatic instructions he was required to follow.

His life had taught him enough about pain to know that there were no guarantees. He could pursue something meaningful and still encounter disappointment, allow himself to be seen and still experience criticism, or give his attention to a talent and discover that developing it required more patience than he originally expected. Yet the possibility of difficulty did not automatically mean that he should refuse the possibility of growth, because Joseph was beginning to understand that avoiding every risk could eventually become another form of losing something before he had ever allowed himself the opportunity to discover it.

The more Joseph considered the future, the more he realized that he no longer wanted to measure his life only by what had happened to him. The divorce, the losses, the difficult seasons, and the fears he continued carrying were all part of his story, but they were not the only parts of him that remained. His ability to imagine, to create, to care, and to develop had survived every season that had once made him wonder whether he would ever feel like himself again. Those parts of Joseph deserved to be explored with the same seriousness he had once given to every possible reason they might fail.

Joseph was not becoming fearless, and he did not need to pretend that he was. What mattered was that he was beginning to understand that fear did not have to disappear before he allowed himself to reveal what had remained within him. He could carry uncertainty into something new without allowing uncertainty to decide that he should never begin, and he could acknowledge the parts of his history that still affected him while believing that his future contained more than a continuation of everything he had already endured.

As he stood within that understanding, Joseph realized that the most important change was not that his past had become easier to explain. The change was that he was no longer waiting for his past to give him permission to have a future. He had spent enough time believing that healing had to be complete before growth could begin, but he was beginning to see that growth itself could become part of the journey toward healing. His talent did not need to wait for every difficult part of his life to become resolved, because there was no guarantee that life would ever become completely free from uncertainty.

For Joseph Bennett, the future was no longer defined by the question of whether fear would continue appearing. He knew that it probably would, because fear had become familiar enough to return whenever something meaningful required him to take a risk. The more important question was what he would choose when it did, because beneath everything he had experienced, beneath every loss and uncertainty that had shaped his understanding of the world, he still possessed the ability to decide whether fear would remain the voice that determined how much of his life he was willing to reveal.

Beneath everything he had survived, Joseph was still there.

Joseph could not rewrite what had already happened, and he could not guarantee what would happen next, but he could begin meeting the future as the person he was rather than waiting for the impossible promise of becoming someone who had never been afraid.

Beneath everything he had survived, Joseph was still there, and beneath everything that remained uncertain, his choice was still waiting for him.
