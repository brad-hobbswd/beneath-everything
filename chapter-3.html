Chapter Three

The Things He Could Not Hold Together

Some endings remain present long after we have accepted that something has changed.

Joseph had always understood, at least in some distant and abstract way, that relationships could end even when a person had once believed they would last. He had known other people who had gone through divorce, had listened to conversations about marriages that had changed beyond repair, and had watched lives take directions that no one involved had originally imagined. None of that, however, prepared him for the moment when he realized that he would have to understand divorce from the inside rather than from a distance, because knowing that relationships sometimes ended was very different from accepting that his own marriage had reached a point from which it would not return.

The end had not arrived in a single moment that Joseph could easily identify. Like many difficult changes in life, it had developed through experiences, conversations, disappointments, and the gradual recognition that wanting something to work was not always enough to restore what had already changed. By the time Joseph accepted that the marriage was over, he had already spent time trying to understand what had happened and wondering whether there had been another way forward, but eventually the reality of the situation became impossible to ignore. Even after he accepted that the relationship had reached its conclusion, however, he had no understanding of what would be required to bring the marriage to a legal end, because divorce was not something he had ever expected to learn how to navigate.

When the process began, Joseph quickly discovered that there was a difference between knowing that he needed to file for divorce and understanding how to move through the system that followed. There were documents that required information, procedures that had to be followed, and legal language that seemed written for people who already understood what each part of the process meant. Joseph did not have that knowledge, and although he tried to learn what was expected of him as he moved forward, he often felt as though he was attempting to understand an unfamiliar language while making decisions that carried permanent consequences for his life.

Some endings could remain present in a person's life long after they had emotionally accepted that the relationship itself was over.

The process became longer and more complicated than he had originally expected, particularly when he learned that there was a problem with the writing of the petition. Something had not been prepared correctly, and what Joseph had hoped would move the process toward its conclusion became another point of delay and uncertainty. He did not immediately understand what had gone wrong or why the language of the petition had created a problem, but the situation placed him in the uncomfortable position of having to correct something within a process he had never wanted to understand in the first place.

The divorce continued to take time, and Joseph learned that some endings could remain present in a person's life long after they had emotionally accepted that the relationship itself was over. Life continued around him because responsibilities did not disappear while legal matters remained unresolved, and Joseph still had to wake each morning, make decisions, care about the people in his life, and continue moving forward while waiting for the process to reach its final conclusion. The experience taught him that an ending could be both finished and unfinished at the same time, because although the relationship had changed beyond repair, the legal process continued requiring his attention until everything had finally been brought to its proper conclusion.

During this period, Joseph faced another experience that would become deeply connected to his understanding of loss. His dog needed veterinary care, and Joseph wanted to make certain that he received the treatment he needed. Because of the circumstances surrounding the care and the involvement of his former partner, the dog was being cared for through arrangements connected to his former partner's home while the necessary veterinary attention was being provided. Joseph trusted that the people involved were doing what was needed, and although information was shared with him about the process, he did not fully understand the significance of every decision being made.

From Joseph's perspective, his dog was receiving medical care, and he believed that the arrangements surrounding that care were connected to making sure the animal was properly looked after. He did not realize that, while he understood the situation as temporary and related to veterinary treatment, steps were being taken that would eventually place his dog under someone else's registration rather than his own. The information available to him did not cause him to recognize what was happening at the time, and it was only after the circumstances had changed that he understood how much had taken place without his full awareness.

When Joseph finally recognized what had happened, the experience became much more than a disagreement about registration or paperwork. His dog had been part of his life and part of the ordinary routines that become deeply meaningful through repetition, companionship, and familiarity. There had been moments of comfort that did not require conversation and a presence within his life that had become so familiar that Joseph had not always considered how much it meant until he was confronted with the possibility of no longer having it in the same way. The loss therefore reached beyond the practical circumstances that created it, because Joseph was grieving a relationship with an animal he loved while also trying to understand how events had unfolded in a direction he had not anticipated.

Looking back on the circumstances, Joseph naturally wondered whether there were questions he might have asked or details he might have understood differently, particularly because he now possessed information that had not been available to him when the events were taking place. The difficulty was that hindsight made the past appear clearer than it had actually been, and Joseph had to remind himself that understanding something afterward did not mean he had possessed that understanding while the situation was still unfolding.

Understanding something afterward did not mean he had possessed that understanding while the situation was still unfolding.

At the time, Joseph had trusted the information he had been given and believed that his dog was receiving the care he needed. Once he understood more about what had happened, he was left with the painful reality that knowledge gained after an event could not return a person to the moment before it occurred. He could wish that circumstances had unfolded differently without pretending that he had known everything that would eventually be revealed.

The divorce and the loss of his dog were different experiences, but they became connected within Joseph's life because both forced him to live through endings that did not unfold according to the future he had once imagined. In one situation, he entered a legal process without knowing what would be required of him and encountered complications that extended the journey toward its conclusion. In the other, he believed his dog was receiving necessary care while circumstances were developing in ways he did not fully understand until afterward. Both experiences left him facing a future that looked different from the one he had previously expected.

Over time, Joseph began to recognize that peace might not come from discovering a perfect explanation for every painful event. There were things he wished he had known, and there were moments he would have approached differently if he had possessed the knowledge he gained later, but wishing that the past had been different did not change the reality of what had occurred. Some questions remained unanswered because life did not always provide an explanation that made loss easier to accept.

This required Joseph to extend himself a kind of grace that had always come more naturally when he offered it to other people. He would not have expected someone entering an unfamiliar divorce process to understand every detail before they encountered it, and he would not have believed that a person who trusted others to provide medical care for an animal they loved should automatically have known about decisions they did not fully understand. Recognizing that he deserved the same patience did not remove his grief, but it began changing the way he lived with it.

Joseph did not become comfortable with what had happened, and neither experience became less painful simply because he began to understand that life had unfolded according to circumstances he could not completely predict. The divorce still represented the end of a relationship he had once expected to continue, while the loss of his dog created an absence that could not be corrected by explaining the circumstances that had led to it. What slowly changed was Joseph's understanding of how he could live after those experiences, because he began to recognize that grief did not require him to remain permanently trapped inside the moment when everything changed.

Acceptance did not require approval.

As the divorce continued toward its eventual conclusion and the loss of his dog remained part of his life, Joseph began learning that acceptance did not require approval. He could acknowledge that something had ended while still grieving what he wished had been different, and he could wish that he had understood more without allowing the past to become the only place where his life continued to exist. The events had happened, and he could not return to them, but the fact that they had changed his life did not mean that they were entitled to determine everything that came after them.

For much of his life, Joseph had believed that understanding was necessary before he could move forward, but this season gradually taught him that some experiences remained difficult even after a person understood everything available to understand. The marriage had ended, the legal process had taken longer than he expected, and his relationship with his dog had changed through circumstances he had not anticipated, but none of those realities erased the possibility that his life could still become something different from what he had imagined.

Joseph was still learning what it meant to move forward without having every answer, and the process did not feel like a single decision that permanently changed the way he thought. It was quieter and more gradual, requiring him to accept that the past could remain meaningful without becoming the only measure of his future. He could grieve what he had lost and still remain open to what had not yet arrived.

The season did not leave Joseph unchanged, but it also did not leave him without hope. He had survived an ending he never expected to face and a loss he had not known how to prepare for, and although he could not yet see what would come next, he was beginning to recognize that surviving difficult experiences did not mean his story had reached its conclusion.

There was still more of his life ahead of him.

There was still more of his life ahead of him, even if he could not yet imagine what it would look like, and somewhere beyond the endings he had spent so long trying to understand, a future was waiting that Joseph had not yet learned how to expect.
